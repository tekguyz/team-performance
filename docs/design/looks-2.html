Team Performance: two more looks to pick from

The app: one screen where the owner or supervisor of a small call center (under 50 people) sees every call credited to the right person and every customer text the rules sent or held back. The feeling: clear and dense without being cold, and fair to the team.

C · Route line

From the signs in a train station: one thick line, round stops, and a plain place where each trip ends.

The signature: a call is drawn as a trip along one line with three stops (the extension is mapped, the call ran 2 minutes or longer, the customer was not asked in 24 hours). A text is sent only if the trip reaches the end. Every skipped text ends at the stop that held it, so "which rule stopped it" is something you see, not something you read. The side menu is the same line with a stop for each screen.

Why it fitsThe supervisor's two questions are "who got credit?" and "why did this survey not go?". The route answers the second at a glance and keeps the numbers plain and dark on white. Plum is calm and is not the alarm colour red, so a Review tag reads as a question. Atkinson Hyperlegible was drawn so every letter and digit is hard to confuse, which a dense table of extensions and times needs.

D · Marked-up report

From a printed report on a supervisor's desk: ruled lines, totals that add up, and a highlighter for what needs a person.

The signature: yellow is a marker, never an alarm. It is used in two places only: a Review row, and the one cell that explains a skipped text (the short length, the customer already asked, the unmapped extension). The Overview totals are written as plain subtraction with dotted leaders, 182 minus 3, 54 and 21 leaves 104, so the numbers can be checked by eye. Offline work is hatched instead of tinted, like a printout.

Why it fitsThe supervisor has to trust the numbers and show them to clients, so the page reads like a document you can sign: arithmetic that adds up, black type on white, nothing glowing. Yellow marks a question for a person, so a flag feels like a note in the margin, never a red verdict on someone's work. Bitter is a sturdy, friendly slab; Source Sans 3 is quiet and clear in dense columns.
