Team Performance: two looks to pick from

The app: one screen where the owner or supervisor of a small call center (under 50 people) sees every call credited to the right person and every customer text the rules sent or held back. The feeling: clear and dense without being cold, and fair to the team.

Both looks show the same sample day at Coral Line Support: the Overview, a Team row flagged for Review with its drawer open, the Surveys log with the drawn phone, and the landing page's first screen. Pick one. The logo comes next, on its own page.

A · Harbor chart

From the nautical charts every South Florida boater knows: white water, pale shallows, magenta only for the marks that matter.

The signature: call volume across the day is drawn like depth shading, busier hours in deeper blue with each hour's count set in italic like a chart sounding. A dashed magenta line marks a busy hour. Every survey state reads by its line as well as its colour: solid for sent, dashed for under 2 minutes, dotted for already asked, struck for no match.

Why it fitsA supervisor glancing up from a busy floor needs to see what moved and where to look. Calm blues carry the data, magenta is kept for the one thing that needs a person (a Review flag, the main button), so nothing on screen shouts at the team. Archivo is a plain, sturdy working face with real italics for the figures.

B · Box score

From the sports page box score: every player credited for what they did, in tight columns with a total at the bottom.

The signature: the day's calls read as a line score, one column per hour like innings, with the totals boxed at the end and the open flags in the team colour. The Team table is a box score with a team total row. Survey states read by mark as well as colour: solid for sent, open square for under 2 minutes, dot for already asked, struck for no match.

Why it fitsThe product's whole job is giving each person credit, and a box score is the most familiar credit sheet there is. White ground and black type keep a dense table easy to read in a bright office. Coral orange is Coral Line's team colour, used for the main button and for flags; tall condensed caps give the headings energy without making the numbers feel like a scoreboard to fear.
